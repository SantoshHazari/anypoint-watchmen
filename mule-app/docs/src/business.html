Executive summary

MuleSoft is licensed by capacity: flows, messages, data throughput, API instances and more. The bill reflects what was consumed, but it arrives after the fact. By then an overage has already happened, and any risky change made in the platform has already gone unnoticed for weeks.

Anypoint Watchmen is a small, self-hosted monitoring application that closes that gap. Every hour it reads the organisation's usage, audit log and inventory from Anypoint Platform. It compares consumption with the contracted limits and alerts the right people by email when something needs attention.

In one sentence: Watchmen turns the monthly invoice and an unread audit log into a live dashboard and early-warning emails, so licence and governance decisions are made before they become problems.

What the business gets

  • Cost control: early warning before contract limits are breached, and time to act (optimise, re-plan, or negotiate) instead of paying overage.
  • Governance and security: deletions of applications, APIs, environments, policies or connected apps are surfaced within the hour.
  • Better renewals: real consumption trends (peak and cumulative) to right-size the next contract.
  • Less manual effort: no more monthly spreadsheet reconciliation or log trawling.

What it does

Watchmen has four capabilities. All of them run automatically every hour and can also be started on demand from the dashboard.

1Licence & entitlement tracking

Reads consumption for every usage meter Anypoint exposes and compares it with the limits on the contract.

  • Contract entitlements (for example Mule Flows, Mule Messages, Data Throughput, API Manager instances, Flex Gateway calls, IDP pages) show used / limit / % and a traffic-light status.
  • Understands both licence models: high watermark (the peak counts) and drawdown (a pool used up over the contract).
  • Monitoring-only metrics (such as LLM token usage) are shown for awareness and never raise alerts.

2Audit log watch

Reads the platform audit log and keeps only the changes that matter.

  • Logins, views, searches and permission changes are excluded before they are downloaded.
  • Each remaining change is rated Critical, Warning or Info using business-defined rules.
  • Only new events are fetched each hour, so nothing is missed or double counted.

3Alerts & notifications

One alert list for both licence and audit risks, with a simple lifecycle.

  • Open → Acknowledged → Resolved, managed from the dashboard.
  • Licence alerts escalate (Warning → Critical) and close themselves when usage drops back.
  • Audit alerts close themselves after 7 days (configurable).
  • New alerts are emailed to a recipient list that business users can edit themselves.

4Platform inventory

A current list of what exists in the organisation.

  • Business groups and their hierarchy.
  • Environments (production vs. sandbox).
  • Deployed CloudHub 2.0 applications and their status and runtime version.
  • API Manager instances per environment.

Use cases

Each scenario below shows how Watchmen changes the outcome of a situation that platform teams meet regularly.

1. Avoiding a message overageFinance · Platform owner

Situation

A new integration goes live and message volume grows faster than planned. The drawdown pool will run out before the contract ends.

What Watchmen does

When Mule Messages reaches 80% of the pool, a Warning email goes out. At 95% the alert escalates to Critical.

Outcome

The team has weeks, not days, to optimise the integration or agree a top-up at a negotiated price instead of paying overage.
2. Catching a risky changeSecurity · Governance

Situation

Someone deletes a Connected App or an API policy, by mistake or deliberately.

What Watchmen does

The change is rated Critical within the next hourly cycle. It is emailed with who did it, what and when.

Outcome

Incidents are detected the same day rather than when a consumer reports a failure. Accountability is clear.
3. Right-sizing the renewalProcurement · Leadership

Situation

The MuleSoft contract is up for renewal and the vendor proposes new capacity figures.

What Watchmen does

Provides month-by-month history of every meter: peaks for high-watermark items and cumulative use for drawdown items.

Outcome

The negotiation is based on evidence. Over-provisioned items can be reduced and under-provisioned ones planned for.
4. Reconciling the invoiceFinance

Situation

The monthly MuleSoft usage summary arrives and someone has to check it.

What Watchmen does

Shows the same metrics, in the same order as the invoice, with Watchmen's own measured values.

Outcome

Differences are spotted quickly. Metrics not on the contract are visible but clearly separated.
5. Keeping demo and sandbox estates tidyPresales · Platform owner

Situation

Demo apps and API instances pile up across business groups and quietly consume entitlements.

What Watchmen does

The inventory lists every app and API instance by business group and environment, next to the entitlement it consumes.

Outcome

Unused items are found and removed, which frees capacity for real work.
6. Audit and compliance evidenceCompliance · Internal audit

Situation

An auditor asks for evidence that changes to production integrations are monitored.

What Watchmen does

Keeps a classified log of significant changes and a record of every alert and how it was handled.

Outcome

Evidence is available immediately, with acknowledgement and resolution history.

Scope, limits and roadmap

In scope today

  • One Anypoint organisation, including its business groups.
  • Hourly collection of usage, audit log and inventory.
  • Contract limits and alert thresholds held in configuration files.
  • Email alerts. Recipients and audit settings are editable in the dashboard.
  • Runs on a laptop/server (Anypoint Studio or Mule runtime) or in CloudHub 2.0.

Good to know

  • Anypoint usage data arrives with roughly a 3-day delay. That is fine for licence tracking but not real-time.
  • Some usage meter names must be confirmed once against the organisation's Anypoint account during setup.
  • The dashboard has no login of its own yet. Keep it on an internal network.
  • Limits must be updated when the contract changes.
